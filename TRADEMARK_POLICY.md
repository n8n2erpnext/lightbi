# LightBI Trademark and Branding Policy

Status: project policy
Date: 2026-09-01
Scope: use of the LightBI name, logos, product identity, and claims of official origin

## Purpose

LightBI is open-source software. The source license grants rights to use, study, modify, and redistribute the software under its terms. Those rights do not by themselves grant a right to misrepresent a modified build, service, package, website, account, or distribution as an official LightBI release or as endorsed by the LightBI project.

This policy separates source-code freedom from product identity. The LightBI name, logos, distinctive product artwork, and phrases that imply official origin are project marks to the extent protected by applicable law.

## Uses that are welcome

Truthful descriptive and nominative references are welcome. Examples include:

- `Based on LightBI`;
- `Fork of LightBI`;
- `Built from the LightBI source code`;
- factual discussion, reviews, tutorials, screenshots, compatibility notes, and community support;
- links to the official LightBI project while clearly identifying a modified or third-party distribution as independent.

A fork may explain its ancestry and compatibility. It should make the relationship clear enough that a reasonable user will not mistake the fork for an official LightBI release.

## Uses that require different branding

Do not use LightBI branding in a way that falsely suggests official origin, sponsorship, certification, or endorsement. In particular, a modified or independently distributed build should not:

- call itself `Official LightBI`, `LightBI Official`, or an equivalent claim;
- use the LightBI logo or application identity as the primary product identity without clear differentiation;
- use domains, package names, publisher names, signing identities, update channels, account services, or download pages that are likely to be mistaken for official LightBI infrastructure;
- remove or obscure notices that a build is modified, unofficial, or independently distributed when that distinction is material;
- present a locally edited badge, text string, or UI flag as proof that the build was issued or verified by LightBI.

Forks intended for public redistribution should adopt a distinct product name and visual identity. They may retain accurate attribution to LightBI in documentation and about screens.

## What counts as official

A build is not official merely because its source code says that it is. Official origin is determined by independent project authority, not by client-controlled UI.

The long-term LightBI verification model uses release signing, installation attestation, signed entitlement/service authority, and operating-system publisher trust. Until those mechanisms are deployed, LightBI UI must describe verification conservatively and must not claim cryptographic official-build verification.

Official LightBI services may reject modified or unrecognized builds even when those builds remain fully usable under the source license for local Basic functionality.

## No source-license restriction

This policy does not change or narrow the software license. It governs branding and representations of origin, not the right to fork the source. If a use is permitted by the software license but uses different branding and does not mislead users about origin or endorsement, this policy is not intended to prevent that use.
